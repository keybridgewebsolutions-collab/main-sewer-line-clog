**Quick answer:** a main sewer line clog is a blockage in the one large pipe that carries all of your home's wastewater out to the city sewer or septic tank. The giveaway is **several drains backing up at once**, especially water coming up in the tub, shower or basement floor drain when you flush a toilet. Stop using all water right away, don't pour chemical drain cleaner down any drain, and call a plumber. Clearing a main line usually takes a heavy-duty sewer auger or hydro jetting, and often a camera inspection to find the cause.

## Is it a main sewer line clog or just one clogged drain?

This is the first thing to work out, because the fix is completely different.

| What you notice | Likely problem |
|---|---|
| One sink or one toilet is slow, and everything else drains fine | A clog in that fixture's drain. Usually a plunger or small drain snake fixes it. |
| **Several drains are slow or backed up at the same time** | **Main sewer line clog** |
| **Flushing a toilet makes water rise in the tub or shower** | **Main sewer line clog** |
| **Water or sewage comes up through the basement floor drain** | **Main sewer line clog** |
| Gurgling from drains or toilets when another fixture is used | Often a main line clog (sometimes a venting problem) |
| Sewage smell inside, or soggy patches in the yard above the sewer line | Main line clog or a broken sewer pipe |
| Water bubbling out of the outside sewer cleanout | **Main sewer line clog** |

## Signs your main sewer line is clogged

- **Multiple fixtures back up together,** especially toilets, tubs and showers on the lowest floor.
- **Sewage comes out of the shower or tub drain.** These are usually the lowest drains in the house, so wastewater that can't leave through the blocked main line comes up there first.
- **The toilet gurgles or bubbles** when you run the sink or washing machine.
- **The washing machine drains into the tub or floor drain** instead of away.
- **A sewage smell** around drains, the basement or the yard.
- **Unusually green or soggy patches of lawn** along the sewer line's path, which can mean a crack or break.

## What to do right now when the main line backs up

1. **Stop using all water.** Don't flush, shower, run the dishwasher or start the washing machine. Every gallon you use has nowhere to go except back into your house.
2. **Tell everyone in the house,** so nobody flushes by mistake.
3. **Keep children and pets away** from the backup. Sewage carries bacteria and viruses. The EPA warns that sewer overflows that back up into homes cause property damage and threaten public health.
4. **Don't pour chemical drain cleaner** into any drain. It won't clear a main line clog. It sits in the pipe, can damage older pipes, and splashes onto whoever opens the line next, including you or your plumber.
5. **Find your sewer cleanout** (see below), but only open it if you know how. Sewage under pressure can spray out.
6. **Call a plumber.** If the backup is large, water is spreading, or sewage has reached carpet or drywall, you may also need a water damage restoration company and your insurance company.

## What does a sewer cleanout look like, and where is it?

A sewer cleanout is the access point a plumber uses to reach your main sewer line. It's usually:

- **A short pipe sticking out of the ground,** about 3 to 6 inches across, with a screw-on cap. It's often white PVC or black ABS plastic, sometimes cast iron. Look near the foundation on the side of the house closest to the street, or between the house and the street.
- **In the basement, crawl space or garage,** as a capped fitting on the large drain pipe near where it leaves the house. The cap often has a square nut or a recessed plug.
- **Sometimes labeled "clean out" or "CO".** Some homes have two: one near the house and one near the property line.

If water is pooling around the outside cleanout, or the cap looks wet, the clog is past that point, usually between the house and the street.

## Why is sewage coming out of my shower drain?

Because your main sewer line is blocked. When you flush a toilet or run a sink, the wastewater flows down to the main line, hits the blockage and backs up. It comes out of the lowest opening in the house, which is usually a shower, tub or basement floor drain. A plunger in the shower won't fix it, because the blockage isn't in the shower. Stop using water and call a plumber.

## Why is sewage coming out of my outside drain?

If sewage is coming out of your outside sewer cleanout or a yard drain, the main line is blocked further down, between that point and the city sewer or septic tank. It can also mean the **city's sewer main** is blocked, especially if neighbors have the same problem. In that case, call your city or water utility's emergency line as well.

## What causes a main sewer line clog?

- **Tree roots.** Roots grow into small cracks and joints, especially in older clay and cast iron pipes, then fill the pipe. This is one of the most common causes.
- **"Flushable" wipes, paper towels and feminine products.** They don't break down like toilet paper and snag inside the line.
- **Grease, fats and oils** poured down the kitchen sink. They harden and coat the pipe, catching everything else.
- **A sagging or "bellied" pipe,** where part of the line has sunk and waste collects in the low spot.
- **A broken, cracked or collapsed pipe,** often from age, ground movement or roots.
- **Foreign objects,** such as toys or tools flushed by accident.
- **A blocked city sewer main,** which isn't on your property at all.

## How do you get a clog out of the main sewer line?

There are three main methods. A plumber chooses based on what's causing the clog.

1. **Sewer auger (drain snake).** A motorized cable, often 50 to 100 feet or longer, is fed through the cleanout to cut through the clog. This is the most common first step.
2. **Hydro jetting.** A high-pressure water jet scours the inside of the pipe, washing away grease, sludge and roots. It cleans the whole pipe wall, not just a hole through the clog, so the line often stays clear longer.
3. **Camera inspection.** A waterproof camera is run down the line to see exactly what's causing the clog, such as roots, a belly or a break, and where it is. This tells you whether the line just needs clearing or actually needs repair.

If the camera shows a broken or collapsed section, clearing it won't last, and the pipe needs **repair or replacement**, sometimes without digging up the yard (trenchless repair).

## Can I unclog a main sewer line myself?

Some homeowners rent a heavy-duty sewer auger from a tool rental store and clear the line through the cleanout. It can work for a simple clog, but be realistic about the risks:

- Sewage can **spray out** when you open the cleanout if the line is full.
- Heavy augers can **kick, twist and cause injuries** if the cable catches.
- The cable can **get stuck** in roots or a broken pipe, turning a clog into a bigger repair.
- You can't see **why** it clogged, so it may come straight back.

If you're not comfortable with any of that, or the first try doesn't work, call a plumber.

## Can I do hydro jetting myself?

It's not recommended. Hydro jetting uses water at very high pressure, which can cause serious injuries and can **damage older or already cracked pipes**. Plumbers usually run a camera first to check the pipe can take it. Leave hydro jetting to a professional.

## What can I pour down a drain to unclog the sewer line?

Nothing will reliably clear a **main** sewer line clog from a drain. Chemical drain cleaners are made for small, shallow clogs in one fixture, not a blockage of roots or wipes far down the main line. They can harm older pipes and make the job dangerous for whoever clears the line. Enzyme or bacteria-based products can help **prevent** buildup over time, but they won't fix an active backup.

## How to unclog a main sewer line without a cleanout

If your home has no accessible cleanout, plumbers typically get into the line by **removing a toilet** and running the auger through the toilet's drain opening, or through the roof vent. Both are awkward and messy jobs, so this is a good reason to call a professional. Many plumbers will also suggest **installing a cleanout** so future clogs are quicker and cheaper to clear.

## How much does it cost to fix a main sewer line clog?

It depends mostly on **what's causing it and how easy it is to reach**:

- **Clearing a simple clog** through an accessible cleanout costs the least.
- **No cleanout,** clearing through a toilet or roof vent, usually costs more because it takes longer.
- **Hydro jetting** costs more than snaking, but it often keeps the line clear for longer.
- **A camera inspection** is often added to find the cause.
- **Repairing or replacing a broken section** costs the most, and depends on the depth, length and whether the yard has to be dug up.
- **Nights, weekends and emergencies** can cost more.

Ask for the call-out fee and an estimate before work starts, and ask whether a camera inspection is included.

## Who is responsible for a clogged main sewer line?

In most areas, **homeowners are responsible for their sewer line** from the house out to where it connects to the city sewer (the "sewer lateral"), and the **city or utility is responsible for the public sewer main**. Exactly where your responsibility ends varies by city, so check with your local utility. If several neighbors are backing up at the same time, the problem is probably in the city main, so call the utility first.

## Does homeowners insurance cover a sewer backup?

Standard homeowners policies often **don't cover sewer or drain backups** unless you've added a "water backup" or "sewer backup" endorsement. Some policies may cover sudden damage from a broken pipe but not the clog itself. Check your policy, take photos of any damage, and call your insurer quickly.

## How to prevent main sewer line clogs

- **Only flush toilet paper.** Put wipes, even ones labeled "flushable", paper towels and hygiene products in the trash.
- **Keep grease out of the sink.** Let it cool and throw it away.
- **Use drain strainers** in sinks, tubs and showers.
- **Ask about root control** if you have large trees near the sewer line.
- **Schedule a camera inspection** if you've had backups before, or before buying an older home.
- **Ask about a backwater valve** if you're in an area where the city sewer backs up during storms. It helps stop sewage flowing back into your home.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know if my main sewer line is clogged?

Several drains back up at the same time, water rises in the tub or shower when you flush, or sewage comes up through a floor drain. One slow sink on its own is usually just that sink's drain.

### Is a clogged main sewer line an emergency?

Yes, if sewage is backing up into your home. It's unsanitary, can damage floors and walls quickly, and gets worse every time someone uses water. Stop using water and call a plumber.

### Can a main sewer line clear itself?

Rarely, and not for long. Even if water slowly drains away, the blockage (roots, grease or wipes) is still there and the backup usually comes back, often at a worse moment.

### How long does it take to clear a main sewer line?

A straightforward clog cleared through a cleanout often takes an hour or two once the plumber arrives. No cleanout, heavy roots, or a camera inspection and repair take longer.
